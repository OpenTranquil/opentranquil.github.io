## Design Context

### Users

TranquilOS serves systems programmers, operating-system researchers, contributors, and technically sophisticated evaluators. They arrive to understand the architecture, verify engineering depth, build or download the system, and navigate into the documentation without marketing friction.

### Brand Personality

Quiet, exacting, and architectural. The interface should feel calm and authoritative: a digital exhibition for a serious engineering artifact, with editorial restraint and the precision of an instrument panel.

### Aesthetic Direction

Museum-grade technical editorial design informed by Leonardo da Vinci's engineering notebooks at a structural—not historical—level: book typography, compact proportional grids, rigorous marginal indexing, and diagrams that reveal how the system is reasoned about. Keep the palette modern and neutral with mineral blue used sparingly; do not use parchment colors, sepia ink, simulated stains, distressed textures, handwriting fonts, or ornamental sketches. Avoid generic SaaS cards, loud gradients, glassmorphism, neon cyberpunk, excessive rounding, and decoration without meaning.

### Design Principles

1. Treat engineering content as the exhibit: typography, diagrams, and spatial composition lead.
2. Build hierarchy through scale, rhythm, and hairline rules rather than stacked containers or shadows.
3. Make precision visible through indices, coordinates, metadata, and a consistent editorial grid.
4. Keep color rare and purposeful; mineral blue marks action, focus, or system flow.
5. Preserve speed, readability, bilingual parity, keyboard access, and reduced-motion support.
